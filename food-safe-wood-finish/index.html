Food-safe wood finish for cutting boards: oil, wax or both?

Wood that touches food needs a finish that stays put, has no smell and never turns sticky. Here is how cutting board oil and board wax compare, and which to use when.

By Oakmere Wood Care. Updated November 2026.

Furniture finishes like varnish, lacquer and polyurethane build a hard film on top of the wood. That is great on a dresser, but on a cutting board a knife cuts through the film, and the film chips. Boards, butcher block and wooden utensils are better served by finishes that soak in and can be renewed at home in minutes: oil and wax.

The two finishes that suit cutting boards

Cutting board oilBoard wax / conditioner
What it isFood-grade mineral oil, sometimes with a little beeswaxA thicker balm of beeswax and food-grade mineral oil
How it worksSoaks deep into dry woodFills the surface pores and helps water bead
Best forNew, very dry or end-grain boardsSealing after oiling and regular upkeep
Feel after buffingSatin, slightly darker woodSmooth, soft sheen

Most boards do best with both: oil to hydrate, then wax to seal. Our guide to cutting board wax and board butter explains the order in more detail.

Why not olive oil or vegetable oil?

Cooking oils are food, and like any food they can spoil. Inside the wood they oxidise over time, which can leave a board smelling stale and feeling tacky. Food-grade mineral oil (USP) is clear, unscented and does not go rancid, which is why it is the standard choice for cutting boards and butcher block. Every Oakmere product is made with it.

What to look for on the label

  • Food-grade ingredients, named plainly. For mineral oil, look for USP grade.
  • A short ingredient list. Oil, wax, and not much else.
  • No added fragrance, so nothing transfers to food.
  • No film-forming finishes such as varnish on surfaces you cut on.

How to use each one

Oil: pour a little onto a clean, dry board and spread it over every surface, including the edges and end grain. Let it soak in for a few hours or overnight, then wipe off the excess. A new board may take several coats over a few days. See how to season a new cutting board.

Wax: once the board is oiled, rub in a thin layer of conditioner, wait about 20 minutes, then buff away whatever has not absorbed.

Keeping it food-safe day to day

Wash wooden boards by hand in hot, soapy water, rinse, and dry them standing upright. Never soak them or put them in the dishwasher, which is the fastest way to crack a board. Re-oil or re-wax whenever the wood looks pale or dry.
