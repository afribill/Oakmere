What it does

Oakmere Butcher Block Conditioner keeps wooden cutting boards, butcher block and kitchen tools from drying out, cracking and soaking up stains. It is a soft board butter made with Canadian beeswax and food-grade mineral oil (USP).

The oil soaks in to keep the wood conditioned, and the beeswax seals the surface so water beads instead of soaking in. Mineral oil does not go rancid, so there is no stale or fishy smell building up in your board, and nothing is added for scent.

Where to use it

  • Cutting boards
  • Butcher block counters
  • Salad bowls
  • Wooden spoons
  • Serving boards
  • Rolling pins

How to use it.

All you need is a soft, clean cloth.

  1. Wash and dry

    Hand wash the board, then let it dry completely, standing on its edge.

  2. Work it in

    Rub a layer over every face and edge, end grain included.

  3. Let it soak

    Leave it at least 20 minutes, or overnight for a new or very dry board.

  4. Wipe off

    Buff away anything that has not absorbed, and the board is ready.

Questions

Is the Butcher Block Conditioner safe for food surfaces?

It is a food-safe conditioner made with canadian beeswax and food-grade mineral oil (usp). Let it absorb, buff off the excess, and the board is ready to use.

What is the difference between the Cutting Board Oil and the Butcher Block Conditioner?

The oil is a pourable blend of food-grade mineral oil with a little beeswax. It soaks deep into dry wood, so it suits new, very dry or end-grain boards. The conditioner is a thicker wax balm that seals the surface and helps water bead. Most boards do best with oil first, then conditioner. See oil vs wax.

How often should I condition a cutting board?

About once a month for a board in daily use, or whenever it looks lighter, feels dry, or water stops beading on it.

The balm looks creamy rather than golden. Is that normal?

Yes. Natural beeswax blended with oil sets to a soft, creamy colour in the tin. It goes on clear and buffs to a warm glow.

My tin softened in a hot car. Is it ruined?

No. Beeswax softens in heat and firms up again once it cools. Store the tin closed, away from direct heat and sun.
