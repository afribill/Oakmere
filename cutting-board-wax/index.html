Cutting board wax and board butter: what they are and how to use them

Board butter, cutting board wax and butcher block conditioner are names for the same idea: a soft blend of wax and oil that seals a wooden board after it is oiled.

By Oakmere Wood Care. Updated November 2026.

Wooden cutting boards need two things to last: oil to keep the wood from drying out and cracking, and a little wax to slow down how fast water and food juices get back in. Board butter, cutting board wax and butcher block conditioner all describe that second step. They are soft blends of wax and oil that you rub on and buff off.

Oil first, then wax

Oil is thin. It soaks deep into the wood fibres and replaces the moisture that washing and drying pull out. Wax is thicker. It stays closer to the surface and fills the pores, which helps water bead up instead of soaking in. The two work best together:

  • Thirsty or brand-new board: oil it first, often several times, with a thin oil like Oakmere Cutting Board Oil.
  • Then seal: finish with a coat of conditioner like Oakmere Butcher Block Conditioner.
  • Regular upkeep: once a board is seasoned, the conditioner alone is usually enough.

What goes into a good board butter

For a surface you cut food on, the ingredient list should be short and food-grade. Oakmere Butcher Block Conditioner is made with Canadian beeswax and food-grade mineral oil (USP), and it is unscented. Because mineral oil stays stable, there is no stale smell building up in the board over time.

How to apply butcher block conditioner

  1. Wash and dry

    Wash the board by hand, then let it dry completely, standing on its edge so both faces get air.

  2. Rub it in

    Scoop a little conditioner with a clean cloth or your fingers and work it over every face, edge and end grain.

  3. Let it sit

    Leave it for at least 20 minutes, or overnight for a very dry board. The warmth of a room helps the oil soak in.

  4. Buff off

    Wipe away anything that has not absorbed and buff with a clean cloth until the surface feels smooth, not greasy.

How often

About once a month for a board in daily use, and whenever the wood looks lighter in colour, feels dry or water stops beading on it. End-grain boards and butcher block counters are thirstier and may need it more often at first.

Not just cutting boards

The same conditioner works on butcher block counters, wooden salad bowls, serving boards, rolling pins and wooden spoons. Anything wooden that gets washed regularly benefits from it. For the full routine, see how to care for a cutting board.
