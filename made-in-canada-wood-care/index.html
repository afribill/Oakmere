Why wood care matters in a Canadian home

Canadian homes put wood through a lot. Long heating seasons make indoor air very dry in winter, while humid summers swing the other way. Wood takes on and gives off moisture with the seasons, and when it dries out it can look pale and dull, and in some cases develop small cracks or loose joints. Keeping furniture and cutting boards conditioned helps them handle those swings, and the buffed wax layer helps the surface resist everyday spills and fingerprints.

A good rhythm for most Canadian homes:

  • Late fall, once the heat comes on: condition furniture and boards that are prone to drying out.
  • Mid-winter: check high-use pieces such as dining tables and cutting boards, and give them another coat if they look pale.
  • Spring: a full polish as part of spring cleaning.
