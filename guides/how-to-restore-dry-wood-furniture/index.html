How to restore dry wood furniture without refinishing

Most tired furniture is not ruined, just dry. Before you sand, strip or replace it, try conditioning it. Here is how to tell what a piece needs and how to bring it back.

By Oakmere Wood Care. Updated November 2026.

A dull, pale or patchy table often looks like it needs refinishing. Very often it just needs moisture and a fresh wax layer. Conditioning is quick, cheap and reversible, so it is always worth trying first. This guide explains how to tell dry wood from a damaged finish and how to restore it.

First: dry wood or damaged finish?

What you seeLikely causeWhat to do
Pale, dull, “thirsty” look, but smooth to the touchDried-out wood and worn waxCondition and polish (this guide)
Fine surface scratches and dull rings from useWorn top layerCondition and polish; light marks often blend in
Finish peeling, flaking or crazed into tiny cracksFinish breaking downConditioning helps the look; a refinish fixes it
Bare, grey wood with no finish leftFinish worn awayCondition generously; consider refinishing for heavy use

How to restore dry wood

  1. Clean off the grime

    Years of dust, kitchen grease and old polish dull the surface. Wipe the piece with a cloth barely damp with mild soapy water, working in sections, and dry each section right away.

  2. Let it dry fully

    Give the wood an hour or so to dry completely at room temperature before you condition it.

  3. Apply a generous coat

    Work a beeswax conditioner into the wood with the grain. On very dry wood you will see it soak in; add a little more to areas that soak it up quickly.

  4. Leave it longer

    Let it sit for 20 to 30 minutes so the oil can penetrate, then wipe off anything that has not absorbed.

  5. Buff

    Buff with a clean, soft cloth until the surface feels smooth and dry.

  6. Repeat the next day

    Neglected pieces often look much better after a second thin coat the following day, once the first has settled in.

Why this works

Wood takes on and gives off moisture with the seasons. Dry indoor air, especially during long Canadian heating seasons, pulls moisture out and leaves the surface looking pale. The oil in a conditioner such as Oakmere Beeswax Wood Polish & Conditioner soaks into the wood and deepens the colour again, while the beeswax and carnauba leave a thin, buffed layer that brings back the sheen and helps resist everyday moisture.

When a piece really needs refinishing

Conditioning improves the look of almost any wood, but it cannot rebuild a finish that has failed. If the finish is peeling, sticky, or worn through on a surface that gets heavy use, a refinish is the long-term fix. If you might refinish a piece later, see choosing a furniture polish for what to avoid in the meantime.

Keeping it looking good

Once restored, dust regularly and polish every few months, following our furniture polishing routine. Keep pieces out of direct sun and away from heating vents, and use coasters and placemats on tables.
