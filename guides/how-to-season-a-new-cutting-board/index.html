How to season a new cutting board or butcher block

A new board is at its thirstiest. A few rounds of oil, then a coat of conditioner, set it up for years of use. Here is the routine, step by step.

By Oakmere Wood Care. Updated November 2026.

Many new boards arrive with little or no finish. If you start cutting on bare, dry wood, it soaks up water and food juices and is more likely to stain, warp or crack. Seasoning fills the wood with oil first, so there is less room for water to get in.

What you need

  • A food-grade cutting board oil, such as Oakmere Cutting Board Oil
  • A board conditioner, such as Oakmere Butcher Block Conditioner
  • Clean, lint-free cloths or paper towels
  • A few days. Most of the time is waiting.

Seasoning, step by step

  1. Wash and dry

    Wash the board with warm, soapy water to remove dust from the shop, then let it dry completely, standing on its edge, for several hours.

  2. Flood it with oil

    Pour a generous amount of oil onto the board and spread it over every face, edge and end grain. The wood should look wet all over.

  3. Let it soak

    Leave it for a few hours, or overnight. Stand it on its edge so both faces can absorb.

  4. Wipe and repeat

    Wipe off any oil sitting on the surface. Repeat the oiling once a day until the wood stops soaking it up, usually three to five coats. End-grain boards may take more.

  5. Seal with conditioner

    Once the board is saturated, rub in a thin layer of conditioner, let it sit for about 20 minutes, then buff away the excess.

Butcher block counters

The same method works for butcher block countertops, island tops and large boards. Work in sections, pay extra attention to the area around the sink and any cut-outs, and keep the counter clear while the oil soaks in. After the first few coats, re-oil whenever the surface looks dry, and add conditioner to the busiest areas more often.

After seasoning

From here, the routine is simple: wash by hand, dry standing up, and condition about once a month, or whenever the wood looks pale or water stops beading. Our full cutting board care guide covers the day-to-day details.
