Wood care myths, and what to do instead

A few habits that seem sensible end up doing wood more harm than good. Here are the six we hear most, and the simple fix for each.

By Oakmere Wood Care. Updated November 2026.

More polish means more shine.

Thin coats win. Extra product sits on the surface, stays soft, turns sticky and collects dust. The shine comes from buffing a thin layer, not from piling more on.

Olive oil is fine for cutting boards.

Cooking oils can go rancid inside the wood and leave a stale smell. Use food-grade mineral oil, which stays stable and unscented.

Silicone sprays protect your furniture.

They give a quick shine, but residue can build up and make refinishing harder later. A silicone-free wax lasts longer between coats.

Wax makes wood waterproof.

Wax helps wood resist moisture, but standing water still does damage. Wipe up spills promptly and use coasters.

Dull, old furniture needs refinishing.

Often it is just dry. Clean it and condition it first. Sanding and refinishing are the last step, not the first.

A board is clean once it is rinsed.

Wash with hot, soapy water, dry it right away, and store it on its edge so both sides can breathe.

A few more things worth knowing

Dusting is part of polishing

Dust is fine grit. Wiping it around on a dry surface can leave fine scratches over time. Dust with a soft, dry or barely damp cloth, and always dust before polishing.

Wood needs less care than you think

Most furniture only needs a wax polish every few months, and a cutting board about once a month. The rest of the time, gentle cleaning is enough. When in doubt, look at the wood: if it looks pale or dry, it is ready.

Heat and sun do more damage than use

Direct sunlight fades wood, and heating vents dry it out. Moving a piece a few feet can do more for it than any product. In dry Canadian winters, conditioning before the heating season helps.

Test before you trust

Antiques, painted pieces and unusual finishes can react differently. A small test on a hidden spot takes two minutes and avoids surprises.
