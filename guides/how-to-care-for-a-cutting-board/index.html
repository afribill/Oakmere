How to care for a wooden cutting board

A good wooden board can last decades. It only needs four habits: wash it by hand, dry it standing up, oil it when it looks thirsty, and never let it soak.

By Oakmere Wood Care. Updated November 2026.

After every use

  1. Scrape and rinse

    Scrape off food bits and rinse the board under warm water.

  2. Wash by hand

    Scrub both sides with hot, soapy water and a brush or sponge, even if you only cut on one side, so the board dries evenly.

  3. Dry right away

    Towel it off, then stand it on its edge or in a rack so air reaches both faces. Boards left flat and wet can warp.

Never put a wooden board in the dishwasher or leave it soaking in the sink. Long contact with water and heat is the fastest way to warp or crack it.

Once a month: oil and condition

Washing pulls moisture out of the wood over time. When the board looks lighter in colour, feels dry, or water soaks in instead of beading, it is time to treat it.

  • Very dry boards: start with a coat of Cutting Board Oil. Spread it over every surface, let it soak in for a few hours, then wipe off the excess.
  • Then seal, or for regular upkeep: rub in a thin layer of Butcher Block Conditioner, wait about 20 minutes, and buff off what has not absorbed.

Treat the edges and end grain too. End grain absorbs the most oil and where cracks usually start.

Stains and smells

For stains or lingering smells such as onion and garlic, sprinkle the board with coarse salt and scrub it with the cut side of half a lemon, then rinse, dry and re-condition. Light knife marks are normal and give a board character; a well-conditioned surface hides them better.

Good habits

  • Many cooks keep a separate board for raw meat, and wash it promptly after use.
  • Store boards where air can circulate, not sealed in a damp cupboard.
  • Keep boards away from the stove, the dishwasher vent and heating vents.
  • Use an unscented, food-grade product so nothing transfers to food.

Signs a board needs attention

A pale, chalky look, a rough or fuzzy surface after washing, water soaking in quickly, or small cracks near the ends. Most of these improve with a round of oil and conditioner. A rough, raised grain can be smoothed with fine sandpaper before oiling. If you have just bought a board, start with how to season a new cutting board.
